\begin{abstract}
Generative recommendation with Semantic IDs (SIDs) ranks items by
accumulating token-level scores, but autoregressive decoding makes
strong teachers costly to serve. Lightweight, verify-free students
reduce inference overhead, yet conventional gold-prefix token
distillation does not directly supervise preferences over complete SID
candidates or their shared-prefix groups. Our diagnostics reveal two structural supervision gaps in conventional gold-prefix token distillation. First, local conditional-distribution agreement does not directly ensure aligned preferences over complete SID candidates. Moreover, teacher and student may assign different probability masses to hierarchical prefix groups. Second, teacher--student relative-score residuals frequently exhibit opposing signs across SID depths, making discrepancy magnitude alone insufficient for identifying directionally favorable corrections. We propose \method\ to organize teacher supervision over complete SID candidates from two complementary views. HServe aligns complete-candidate distributions and their hierarchically consistent prefix marginals. DA-CDRS decomposes candidate score gaps into depth-wise increments and allocates relative-score distillation weights according to the effects of individual teacher-increment corrections. \section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Generative recommendation represents items as sequences of Semantic-ID
(SID) tokens and retrieves them through constrained autoregressive
decoding. TIGER \citep{tiger} ranks complete candidates using
accumulated conditional log-probabilities along their SID paths.
Although effective, repeated Transformer decoding incurs substantial
online cost. Lightweight students such as SID-MLP \citep{sidmlp}
encode user histories once and predict SID tokens with compact heads,
enabling verify-free beam search. Unlike draft--verify alternatives,
these students require no online teacher check, so their
recommendation quality must be improved through training rather than
inference-time correction. Knowledge distillation transfers teacher
preferences to these efficient architectures, but the organization of
supervision remains critical to recommendation quality.

Conventional gold-prefix token distillation matches conditional distributions along the observed SID path, whereas recommendation decisions compare complete candidates scored through accumulated token probabilities. Under limited student capacity and training coverage, local alignment does not directly guarantee complete-candidate preference agreement. This raises a central question: how can the generative structure of SIDs be exploited to transfer complete-candidate preferences more effectively without changing the student's serving architecture?

First, matching local conditional distributions along the observed SID path does not explicitly align teacher and student preferences over complete candidates. This gap motivates complete-candidate distribution matching, particularly under limited student capacity. Beyond item-level preferences, shared SID prefixes organize candidates into nested groups whose probability masses are obtained by aggregating their descendant items. Teacher--student discrepancies may therefore also appear at multiple prefix granularities. Explicit alignment of these hierarchically consistent marginals provides multi-level optimization constraints derived from the same complete-candidate distribution.

Second, the score gap between two complete SID candidates decomposes into depth-wise relative-score increments. Discrepancies between teacher and student increments can have opposing signs and partially cancel. Consequently, a large local error does not necessarily indicate a correction that favors the teacher-preferred ordering. This motivates depth-wise relative-score distillation that evaluates the directional effect of individual teacher-increment corrections instead of allocating supervision solely by discrepancy magnitude.

We propose \method, a structure-aware distillation framework that exploits two complementary views of the same SID candidate preferences. HServe aggregates complete-candidate probabilities into consistent prefix marginals and performs multi-level distribution alignment. DA-CDRS decomposes complete-candidate score relations along SID depths and allocates supervision to selected relative-score increments. Both objectives jointly optimize the same lightweight student using offline teacher knowledge, while preserving its original verify-free inference process.



Because teacher preference is not a relevance oracle, we assess recommendation quality using held-out next-item Recall and NDCG rather than teacher agreement alone. Experiments on Amazon Beauty, Toys, and Video Games
with MLP, GRU, and CNN students examine recommendation utility gains
over token-distilled baselines. Controlled ablations compare the
structural objectives with simpler leaf-only and uniform-weighting
alternatives.

Our contributions are threefold: 1. We identify structural supervision gaps between gold-prefix token matching and complete-candidate preference transfer, supported by candidate-level, hierarchical, and depth-wise diagnostic evidence. 2. We propose \method, which integrates hierarchically consistent candidate-preference alignment with depth-wise relative-score distillation over shared SID candidate scores. 3. We evaluate \method\ across three datasets and three lightweight student architectures, examining recommendation performance and the contributions of its two structural objectives.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Generative Recommendation with Semantic IDs}

Generative recommendation casts retrieval as the generation of
discrete item identifiers. TIGER \citep{tiger} popularized Semantic
IDs (SIDs) constructed through quantization and retrieved through
constrained autoregressive decoding. Subsequent work explores
learnable tokenization and improved item representations, including
ActionPiece \citep{actionpiece}, Learnable Item Tokenization
\citep{letter}, and end-to-end SID learning \citep{etegrec,idgenrec}.
These developments primarily address how identifiers are constructed
and modeled. Other approaches exploit the prefix structure of
generation. RIPOR \citep{ripor}, developed for generative information
retrieval, introduces prefix-oriented ranking optimization. APAO
\citep{apao} targets premature beam pruning through adaptive
prefix-aware optimization in generative recommendation. LOHRec \citep{lohrec} exploits order and hierarchy in quantized identifiers for lightweight sequential recommendation. These works establish that prefixes influence retrieval, rather than merely encoding item identity. ServeSID instead studies teacher-to-student supervision over
complete SID candidates: its hierarchical targets are probability
marginals obtained by aggregating candidates with shared prefixes, not
direct prefix-survival objectives.

\subsection{Knowledge Distillation for Recommendation}

Knowledge distillation transfers teacher predictions or preferences to
compact models \citep{hintonkd}. In recommendation, Ranking
Distillation (RD) \citep{rankdistill} emphasizes highly ranked teacher
items, while Relaxed Ranking Distillation (RRD) \citep{rrd} transfers
relative item orderings through relaxed permutation supervision.
RCE-KD \citep{rcekd} revisits cross-entropy distillation for ranking,
relating its effectiveness to candidate sampling and using
teacher--student-aware selection with adaptive loss combination. These
methods show that recommendation distillation can benefit from
supervision beyond local output matching, but they do not specifically
organize complete SID preferences through shared-prefix marginals and
per-depth score increments.

Generation-oriented distillation offers complementary techniques. GKD
\citep{gkd} uses student-generated trajectories for on-policy
knowledge transfer, whereas DistiLLM-2 \citep{distillm} uses
contrastive supervision over teacher- and student-generated responses.
Both originate in language-model distillation and can be adapted to
generative recommendation. More directly, SID-MLP \citep{sidmlp}
distills autoregressive SID teachers into lightweight position-wise
prediction architectures. SmartGR \citep{smartgr} explicitly
introduces hierarchy-aware SID distillation and beam-aware ranking
distillation, addressing depth-dependent difficulty and prefix
pruning. It is therefore inaccurate to characterize hierarchy or
ranking as unexplored in SID distillation.

SeRveSID instead organizes supervision over shared complete SID candidates in two complementary ways. HServe aligns complete-candidate preferences and their consistent prefix marginals, while DA-CDRS allocates depth-wise relative-score supervision using candidate-level comparisons. Their contributions are examined through leaf-only, individual-component, and uniform-weight ablations.

\subsection{Efficient Serving for Generative Recommendation}

Autoregressive generative recommenders repeatedly evaluate decoding
networks while expanding beams, making inference cost a central
deployment concern. Speculative decoding and draft--verify approaches
accelerate generation through additional proposal or checking stages,
whereas architectural simplification and alternative decoding
strategies reduce per-step overhead. SID-MLP provides a verify-free
route by encoding history once and using lightweight SID prediction
heads with constrained beam search. ServeSID adopts this serving
configuration without claiming a new decoding algorithm: cached
teacher candidates, hierarchical marginals, ranking pairs, and
depth-wise teacher statistics are used during offline distillation and
discarded at deployment. Consequently, its contribution is improving
student training under a fixed inference graph, while offline cache
construction, storage, and training overhead remain separate costs
that should be measured explicitly.

\section{Preliminaries}
\label{sec:prelim}

\subsection{SID Recommendation and Verify-Free Students}
\label{sec:prelim-models}

Let $\mathcal I$ denote the item catalog and $u$ a recommendation
request containing the user's interaction history. Each item
$i\in\mathcal I$ is assigned a length-$L$ Semantic ID (SID),
$z^i=(z_1^i,\ldots,z_L^i)$, whose elements belong to a discrete
codebook. A fixed autoregressive teacher $T$ and a lightweight student
$S$ assign conditional probabilities $p_T$ and $q_S$, respectively.
Their complete-item scores are
\begin{equation}
s_T(i\mid u)=\sum_{d=1}^{L}\log p_T(z_d^i\mid u,z_{<d}^i),\qquad
s_S(i\mid u)=\sum_{d=1}^{L}\log q_S(z_d^i\mid u,z_{<d}^i).
\label{eq:serving-scores}
\end{equation}

Recommendations are obtained by expanding valid SID prefixes with
constrained beam search and ranking completed candidates by these
scores. Unlike the Transformer teacher, the verify-free student
encodes the history once and uses lightweight prediction heads for
successive SID positions. It requires neither teacher evaluation nor
an additional verifier during serving. Both models operate in the same
SID space and obey identical prefix-validity constraints. We keep the
teacher and SID assignment fixed, so differences among trained
students reflect their learned conditional scoring functions rather
than changes to identifier construction.

\subsection{Gold-Prefix Token Distillation Baseline}
\label{sec:prelim-base}

We consider a standard student trained with ground-truth SID
cross-entropy and token-level knowledge distillation:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{base}}
&=\mathcal L_{\mathrm{digit}}
+\lambda_{\mathrm{tok}}\mathcal L_{\mathrm{tok}},\\
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{digit}}
&=-\sum_{d=1}^{L}\log q_S(z_d^{\mathrm{gt}}\mid u,z_{<d}^{\mathrm{gt}}),\\
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{tok}}
&=\sum_{d=1}^{L}\KL\!\left(
p_T(\cdot\mid u,z_{<d}^{\mathrm{gt}})
\,\|\,
q_S(\cdot\mid u,z_{<d}^{\mathrm{gt}})
\right).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:base-obj}
\end{equation}

Here $z^{\mathrm{gt}}$ is the observed next-item SID and
$\lambda_{\mathrm{tok}}$ controls the distillation strength.
Importantly, token KD matches the entire next-code distribution at
each gold-path prefix, not merely the probability of the correct code.
Its direct targets nevertheless remain conditional predictions at
visited prefixes. Because alternative complete candidates may follow
prefixes absent from the observed path, matching these local
distributions does not directly constrain their joint ranking. We use
this objective to define the token-distilled baseline; ServeSID
retains the ground-truth term but replaces the token-KL term with its
structure-aware objectives.

\subsection{Two Structural Views of SID Scoring}
\label{sec:prelim-structure}

\textbf{Hierarchical candidate organization.} A prefix $p$ of length
$d$ identifies the group
\begin{equation}
\mathcal C^{(d)}(p)=\{i\in\mathcal I:z_{1:d}^{i}=p\}.
\label{eq:prefix-group}
\end{equation}
If $p'$ extends $p$, then
$\mathcal C^{(d')}(p')\subseteq\mathcal C^{(d)}(p)$. Thus, complete
candidates form nested groups across SID depths. On a fixed candidate
support, the sum of complete-item preference probabilities within a
group defines its prefix marginal. These marginals are mutually
consistent because each complete SID belongs to exactly one prefix
group at any depth. These groups are induced by SID codes and need not
coincide with externally defined semantic product categories.

\textbf{Additive relative-score composition.} For two complete
candidates $i^+$ and $i^-$, define the score gap for
$m\in\{T,S\}$ as
\begin{equation}
G_m=s_m(i^+\mid u)-s_m(i^-\mid u)=\sum_{d=1}^{L}\Delta G_m^{(d)},
\label{eq:gap-decomp}
\end{equation}
where $\Delta G_m^{(d)}$ is the difference between their conditional
log-probabilities at position $d$. Here depth denotes the SID
position, not a neural-network layer. Increments before the
candidates' first divergent code cancel exactly; later increments may
have opposite signs. The sum, rather than any individual increment,
determines the final relative ordering. These two properties---aggregation across shared SID prefixes and decomposition along generation depths---provide the structural basis for HServe and DA-CDRS.

\section{Diagnosis: Structural Limitations of Token-Level Distillation}
\label{sec:diagnosis}

We analyze token-distilled MLP, GRU, and CNN students on Amazon
Beauty, Toys, and Video Games using 10{,}000 held-out requests per
dataset. The teacher's top-40 valid complete SIDs define a fixed
support for comparing teacher and student scores. No held-out teacher
outputs enter training.

\subsection{Hierarchical Candidate Preference Mismatch}
\label{sec:diag-h1}

Gold-prefix token KL evaluates next-token agreement on the observed
SID path, while complete-candidate KL compares normalized preferences
over the shared support. At prefix depth $d$, we measure hierarchical
drift as
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{HDrift}_d(u)=
\KL\!\left(
\pi_{T,40}^{(d)}(\cdot\mid u)
\;\|\;
\pi_{S,40}^{(d)}(\cdot\mid u)
\right),
\label{eq:hdrift}
\end{equation}
where each marginal sums the probabilities of supported candidates
sharing a prefix.

\textbf{Local-to-candidate preference mismatch.} Gold-prefix token KD aligns conditional distributions along observed SID paths but does not directly match preferences over complete candidates. Figure~\ref{fig:stat-h1}(a) shows that lower token KL need not imply lower candidate-level KL: on Video Games, GRU has lower token KL than MLP ($0.0827$ vs.\ $0.0882$) but higher candidate KL ($0.1020$ vs.\ $0.0768$). This motivates explicit complete-candidate distribution alignment.

\textbf{Hierarchical prefix-preference mismatch.} Shared SID prefixes define nested candidate groups whose probability masses are aggregated from complete candidates (Figure~\ref{fig:hierarchical}). Figure~\ref{fig:stat-h1}(b) shows preferred-prefix disagreement rates of $13.4\%$--$42.8\%$ at depth one and $31.9\%$--$67.7\%$ at depth two. Since exact candidate-distribution matching also determines prefix marginals, hierarchical alignment adds direct multi-granularity optimization constraints rather than independent teacher information.

\subsection{Directionally Mixed Cross-Depth Residuals}
\label{sec:diag-d2}

We examine teacher--student relative-score residuals $r_d=\Delta G_T^{(d)}-\Delta G_S^{(d)}$ on held-out teacher-ordered candidate pairs. Figure~\ref{fig:stat-d12} shows that $75.4\%$--$86.3\%$ of pairs contain both positive and negative depth-wise residuals, while the largest absolute residual is negative in $47.6\%$--$51.3\%$ of cases. Thus, discrepancy magnitude alone does not identify corrections that favor the teacher-preferred ordering. This motivates direction-aware allocation of depth-wise distillation supervision.

Together, hierarchical candidate-preference mismatches and directionally mixed depth residuals motivate the two complementary structural objectives introduced next.

\section{\method: Hierarchy-Aligned and Direction-Aware Distillation}
\label{sec:method}

\method\ transfers teacher knowledge through two complementary
structures of Semantic-ID (SID) scoring. HServe aggregates
probabilities of complete candidates into nested prefix groups, while
DA-CDRS decomposes complete-candidate ranking relations into
depth-wise score increments. Both objectives train the same
lightweight student introduced in Section~\ref{sec:prelim-models};
neither adds prediction heads, alters valid-prefix constraints, or
changes online beam search.

\subsection{Overall Training Objective}
\label{sec:method-total}

For a training request $u$ with observed next-item SID
$z^{\mathrm{gt}}$, our objective is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{\method}}
=\mathcal L_{\mathrm{digit}}
+\lambda_H\mathcal L_{\mathrm{HServe}}
+\lambda_D\mathcal L_{\mathrm{DA\text{-}CDRS}},
\label{eq:total}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal L_{\mathrm{digit}}$ is the ground-truth SID
cross-entropy defined in Section~\ref{sec:prelim-base}, and
$\lambda_H,\lambda_D\ge0$ control the structural objectives. Unlike
the baseline, \method\ does not retain a separate gold-prefix
token-KL term. Ground-truth supervision remains necessary because
matching a teacher's preferences does not guarantee relevance to the
observed next item. The following losses operate on teacher-proposed
complete candidates, with gradients applied only to the student.
Request-level objectives are averaged across the training minibatch, and the teacher is fixed throughout optimization, keeping the distillation targets stationary while the student adapts.

\subsection{HServe: Hierarchical Serving-Distribution Alignment}
\label{sec:method-hserve}

\textbf{Shared complete-candidate support.} For each training
request, we obtain the teacher's top-$M$ beam-reachable, valid
complete SIDs and cache the resulting support $\C_M(u)$. Both models
evaluate these same complete sequences using accumulated conditional
log-probabilities $s_m(i\mid u)$, where $m\in\{T,S\}$. Teacher scores can be precomputed, whereas student scores are recomputed as parameters change. We define a distribution normalized within
this support:
\begin{equation}
\pi_{m,M}(i\mid u)=
\frac{\exp(s_m(i\mid u))}
{\sum_{j\in\C_M(u)}\exp(s_m(j\mid u))},
\qquad i\in\C_M(u).
\label{eq:item-dist}
\end{equation}
This normalization makes teacher and student probabilities comparable
over identical candidates. It represents preference
conditional on the selected support rather than over the entire item
catalog, so candidates outside $\C_M(u)$ are not part of this KL
objective; using a common support avoids interpreting differences
caused merely by two models proposing different candidate lists as
distributional mismatch.

\textbf{Hierarchically consistent prefix marginals.} As illustrated
in Figure~\ref{fig:hierarchical}, shared SID prefixes form nested
candidate groups whose preference mass may differ between teacher and
student. A length-$d$ prefix $p$ defines the subset of supported
candidates whose first $d$ codes equal $p$. We aggregate their complete-candidate
probabilities:
\begin{equation}
\pi_{m,M}^{(d)}(p\mid u)=
\sum_{\substack{i\in\C_M(u)\\z_{1:d}^i=p}}
\pi_{m,M}(i\mid u).
\label{eq:prefix-marginal}
\end{equation}
Every candidate contributes to exactly one prefix group at each
depth, so the marginals are normalized and mutually consistent across
depths. At $d=L$, each distinct complete SID corresponds to a leaf
and the marginal reduces to the complete-candidate distribution. At
shallower depths, it captures the total preference mass assigned to
groups of candidates with common initial codes. No auxiliary prefix
predictor is introduced: all marginals are deterministic aggregations
of the same complete-candidate scores. These groupings are defined by
code identity rather than manually assigned product categories.

\textbf{Multi-level alignment.} HServe minimizes the teacher-to-student
KL divergence at every depth:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{HServe}}(u)=
\sum_{d=1}^{L}\alpha_d\,
\KL\!\left(
\pi_{T,M}^{(d)}(\cdot\mid u)\;\|\;
\pi_{S,M}^{(d)}(\cdot\mid u)
\right),\qquad \alpha_d\ge0.
\label{eq:hserve}
\end{equation}
The hierarchical terms impose optimization constraints at different levels of candidate aggregation. A shallow-prefix KL term penalizes discrepancies in the total preference mass assigned to competing branches, whereas the leaf-level term distinguishes individual candidates within those branches. Through the shared candidate scores, gradients from each prefix marginal propagate to its supported descendants. Consequently, multi-depth alignment changes how the same teacher preference information is emphasized during optimization, without introducing additional prediction heads or independent supervision targets. The leaf term ($d=L$) matches complete-item preferences; earlier terms penalize discrepancies in the mass allocated to shared-prefix groups. The shallower terms contain no independent teacher information---exact leaf agreement implies agreement for every aggregated marginal---and instead impose direct multi-granularity constraints when the student cannot match the teacher exactly. Differentiation through each
student marginal propagates group-level errors to the complete
candidate scores that constitute it. Depth weights $\alpha_d$ specify the
optimization emphasis; they do not change the underlying teacher
distribution.

\subsection{DA-CDRS: Direction-Aware Cross-Depth
Relative-Score Distillation}
\label{sec:method-dacdrs}

\textbf{Cross-cutoff candidate pairs.} Let
$\operatorname{rank}_T(i\mid u)$ denote an item's rank within the
teacher proposal list. We form teacher-ordered pairs
\begin{equation}
\mathcal B(u)=\{(i^+,i^-):
\operatorname{rank}_T(i^+\mid u)\le K_{\mathrm{tr}},\;
K_{\mathrm{tr}}<\operatorname{rank}_T(i^-\mid u)\le B\},
\label{eq:pairs}
\end{equation}
with $B\le M$. These pairs compare teacher-preferred candidates
inside a selected cutoff with candidates outside it; they are not
restricted to the exact $K_{\mathrm{tr}}$-th and
$(K_{\mathrm{tr}}+1)$-th items. The pair labels express the
teacher's relative preference, not observed item relevance; incorrect
teacher rankings can therefore still produce valid training pairs
under this definition. Their score gaps are
\begin{equation}
G_m(b)=s_m(i^+\mid u)-s_m(i^-\mid u)
=\sum_{d=1}^{L}\Delta G_m^{(d)}(b),
\label{eq:pairs-gap}
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
\Delta G_m^{(d)}(b)=
\log q_m(z_d^{i^+}\mid u,z_{<d}^{i^+})-
\log q_m(z_d^{i^-}\mid u,z_{<d}^{i^-}),
\label{eq:pairs-inc}
\end{equation}
and $q_T=p_T$ by notation. These increments measure relative
conditional scores, not independent depth-wise item rankings.

\textbf{Direction-aware depth weighting.} Define the
teacher--student increment residual
\begin{equation}
r_d(b)=\Delta G_T^{(d)}(b)-\Delta G_S^{(d)}(b).
\label{eq:residual}
\end{equation}
If two candidates share the same prefix before depth
$d_{\mathrm{div}}(b)$, their relative increments cancel at earlier
depths. We therefore use the validity mask
\begin{equation}
v_d(b)=\mathbf 1[d\ge d_{\mathrm{div}}(b)].
\label{eq:valid-depth}
\end{equation}
Figure~\ref{fig:ranking_depth} illustrates that replacing a
student increment with its teacher counterpart can move the complete
score gap in either direction. For each eligible depth, we
hypothetically replace only the student's relative-score increment
with the teacher's, giving
\begin{equation}
\widetilde G_{S,d}(b)=G_S(b)+r_d(b).
\label{eq:cf-gap}
\end{equation}
We score how much each replacement moves the complete gap toward the
teacher-preferred direction:
\begin{equation}
A_d(b)=v_d(b)\left[\widetilde G_{S,d}(b)-G_S(b)\right]_+
=v_d(b)\left[r_d(b)\right]_+,
\label{eq:attribution}
\end{equation}
and normalize these scores into detached weights,
\begin{equation}
w_d(b)=\operatorname{stopgrad}\left(
\frac{A_d(b)}{\sum_{t=1}^{L}A_t(b)+\epsilon}
\right).
\label{eq:weights}
\end{equation}
A depth thus receives weight only when its teacher increment moves
the complete score gap in the teacher-preferred direction, and the
weight grows with the magnitude of that favorable residual. Because
$A_d(b)$ reduces to the positive part of the increment residual, this
attribution is an operational positive-residual weighting over the
complete gap rather than a causal estimate of depth importance after
retraining; the ``decision'' it references is the direction of the
complete candidate score gap. If every eligible residual in a pair is
nonpositive, the pair contributes zero DA loss. The weights are
detached during backpropagation but recomputed as student scores
evolve, allowing the allocation of supervision to adapt throughout
training.

\textbf{Scale-normalized correction.} To make residual matching less
sensitive to depth-dependent score scales, let
\begin{equation}
\sigma_d=\max\left(
\operatorname{Std}_{\mathrm{train,valid}}[
\Delta G_T^{(d)}],10^{-4}\right).
\label{eq:scale}
\end{equation}
We use a Huber penalty $\rho_H$ for the signed relative-score
difference:
\begin{equation}
\ell_{\mathrm{DA}}(b)=
\lambda_{\mathrm{corr}}\sum_{d=1}^{L}
w_d(b)\,\rho_H\!\left(
\frac{\Delta G_S^{(d)}(b)-\Delta G_T^{(d)}(b)}{\sigma_d}
\right).
\label{eq:correction}
\end{equation}
The request-level loss averages $\ell_{\mathrm{DA}}$ over
$\mathcal B(u)$, using zero contribution when no pair is available.
Attribution-weighted increment matching thus determines which local
teacher differences receive stronger explicit supervision. Since
$A_d(b)=v_d(b)[r_d(b)]_+$, the weighting is equivalent to normalized
positive-residual weighting over eligible depths; its value relative
to uniform weighting is examined empirically in
Section~\ref{sec:exp-ablation}.

\subsection{Offline Training and Verify-Free Inference}
\label{sec:method-train-inf}

We construct teacher proposal lists, candidate scores, pair relations,
and teacher increment statistics from training requests only. The frozen teacher's data are cached; student scores, marginals, replacements, and attribution weights are computed during optimization. No validation or test request contributes teacher targets to the training loss.

At deployment, all teacher-dependent caches and distillation
computations are discarded. The student retains one history-encoding
pass, depth-wise SID prediction, validity masking, and constrained
beam expansion. Consequently, the deployed student retains the original verify-free inference graph without introducing teacher-dependent scoring, auxiliary prediction heads, or reranking stages. The proposed objectives modify only the offline training procedure and do not constitute a new decoding algorithm. Offline cache
generation, storage, and additional student scoring nevertheless
increase training-side cost; they should be reported separately from
serving latency. The method targets recommendation quality under an
unchanged verify-free inference graph, not a guaranteed preservation
of every teacher Top-$K$ item.

\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:exp}

We evaluate \method\ along three questions: (RQ1) Does structure-aware
distillation improve next-item recommendation across datasets and
student architectures? (RQ2) Does HServe add value beyond
complete-candidate, leaf-only distribution matching? (RQ3) Does direction-aware depth-wise distillation complement hierarchical alignment and outperform uniform depth weighting?

\subsection{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:exp-setup}

\textbf{Datasets and evaluation.} We use Amazon Beauty and Toys from
Amazon Reviews 2014 and Video Games from Amazon Reviews 2023. User
interactions are ordered chronologically, with leave-one-out next-item
evaluation and at most 20 recent interactions retained as request
history. All methods within a dataset share identical preprocessing, splits, SID assignments, and validity constraints. We report Recall@5/10 and NDCG@5/10 against the held-out next item. Recall@$K$ measures whether the held-out next item appears among the top-$K$ recommendations, while NDCG@$K$ additionally accounts for its ranking position. These relevance-based metrics assess recommendation utility independently of teacher--student preference agreement.

\textbf{Teacher and students.} A frozen TIGER teacher provides
supervision in an RQ-VAE SID space with four codes per item and
codebook size 256. We distill the teacher into three lightweight
students: an MLP (1.664M trainable parameters), a GRU with hidden size
384 (1.201M), and a CNN with embedding dimension 256 (1.753M). These
are distinct architectures, not an ordered capacity series. Students
share the teacher's SID inventory and employ constrained beam search
with width 50; the teacher's beam-20 results are reference measurements rather than matched-cost comparisons. Fixing the SID inventory and validity constraints ensures that differences among training objectives are not attributable to changes in item tokenization or admissible candidate sequences. Student beam width is held constant within each architecture.  

\textbf{Training configuration.} For every training request, the
offline teacher cache contains up to $M=64$ valid complete SIDs and
their accumulated scores. HServe aligns distributions on this shared
support with depth weights $\alpha_d=1$ and $\lambda_H=4$. DA-CDRS constructs teacher-ordered cross-cutoff pairs between ranks 1--10 and 11--40 for weighted depth-wise relative-score distillation, with $\lambda_D=1$ and increment-correction weight
$\lambda_{\mathrm{corr}}=31.42$; the uniform variant replaces attribution weights with equal weights over eligible SID depths. Models are trained with learning rate
$5\times10^{-5}$ and seeds 42, 43, 44. All teacher scores, pairs, and
increment-scale statistics originate from training requests only;
validation and test teacher outputs are used exclusively for
independent diagnostics. The base KD objective combines digit CE with
gold-prefix token KL, whereas the \method\ objective combines digit CE
with HServe and DA-CDRS without an additional token-KL term. All
methods follow the same validation-based checkpoint selection
protocol.

\textbf{Baselines.} We compare against representative distillation
objectives: RD and RRD transfer ranking structure; GKD and DistiLLM-2
provide generation-aware distillation; RCE-KD adapts cross-entropy
distillation to ranking objectives; SmartGR and LOHRec exploit SID- or identifier-specific signals. Each method keeps its core supervision principle under the same teacher, tokenizer, backbone, and protocol; adaptations are detailed in the supplementary material. All baseline adaptations use the same next-item evaluation protocol and validation-based checkpoint selection rule. This controls for differences in student architecture and evaluation setup when comparing supervision objectives derived from ranking-based, generation-based, and SID-specific distillation methods.

\subsection{Training Cost and Scope}
\label{sec:exp-cost}

\method\ changes only the offline training objective. The deployed
student retains the same history encoder, SID prediction heads,
validity masking, and constrained beam search as its baseline
counterpart, with no teacher queries or additional reranker.
Therefore, the proposed distillation losses do not introduce extra
serving-time model components. Any throughput or latency advantage
relative to the autoregressive TIGER teacher is primarily attributable
to the lightweight student architecture rather than to a newly
accelerated decoder.

The efficiency trade-off is instead concentrated offline. Teacher
candidate generation and caching, evaluation of complete candidates
during student training, construction of cross-cutoff pairs, and
calculation of depth attribution all add training-side computation or
storage. These costs should be distinguished from online inference
costs, and numerical claims about identical latency or overall
efficiency require matched-hardware measurements. Our empirical scope
is further restricted to one frozen TIGER teacher, one SID tokenizer,
and three Amazon categories. HServe aligns preferences only within
teacher-proposed supports, while DA-CDRS supplies an
optimization-based rather than causal depth attribution.

\section{Conclusion and Limitations}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We investigate structured candidate-preference distillation for lightweight SID recommendation. \method\ exploits two complementary structures of complete-candidate preferences: hierarchical prefix-group organization and depth-wise relative-score decomposition. HServe aligns preference distributions across SID granularities, while DA-CDRS allocates supervision to selected relative-score increments. Both objectives jointly train a single lightweight student without modifying its verify-free inference architecture.

Our evaluation covers one teacher family, one SID tokenizer, and three product categories. Teacher-proposed supports may omit relevant items, and the positive-residual weighting is a detached operational rule that does not explicitly consider the complete student score gap and is not a causal measure of depth importance. Broader teacher, tokenizer, and deployment settings remain important directions for future study.
