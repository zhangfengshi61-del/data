\begin{abstract}
Semantic-ID (SID) recommenders rank items by accumulating scores
along identifier paths. Distilling their autoregressive decoder into
lightweight students must transfer preferences among complete items,
whereas gold-prefix token KD supervises the observed SID path.
We study this gap through probability masses over shared-prefix groups
and depth-wise complete-pair score differences. \method\ combines
two offline objectives on teacher-proposed complete candidates.
HServe aligns the candidate distribution and its nested prefix
marginals, emphasizing coarse-to-fine SID preferences.
DA-CDRS evaluates how replacing one student score increment with a
teacher increment changes a pairwise ranking surrogate and uses
positive reductions as detached weights for relative-score matching.
A frozen T5 history encoder is retained at inference; trainable
context readers and prefix-conditioned MLP heads replace repeated
teacher-decoder evaluation. Across Beauty, Toys, and Video Games,
three student variants improve next-item NDCG@10 by 1.61\%--5.13\%
relative to the strongest compared non-\method\ baseline.
\par\smallskip\noindent\textbf{Relevance.}
This work addresses personalized item retrieval on the Web:
transferring complete-item preferences to a lightweight SID decoder
without introducing an additional online scoring stage. It relates
to the \emph{Search, Recommendation, and Retrieval-Augmented AI}
track and personalized user modeling.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Semantic-ID (SID) generative recommenders map items to discrete
code paths and score completed identifiers to retrieve a user's next
item. A TIGER teacher \citep{tiger} uses an autoregressive Transformer
decoder; lightweight SID-MLP-style students \citep{sidmlp} retain the
frozen T5 history encoder but replace repeated teacher decoding with
a trainable context reader and four prefix-conditioned MLP heads.
The MLP, GRU, and CNN variants differ only in their context readers.
Our goal is to improve their training supervision without adding
online decoding components.

Gold-prefix token KD trains conditionals on the observed next-item
SID, while recommending requires comparing alternative complete
candidates. The difference is visible in our Video Games diagnostic:
a GRU has lower gold-prefix token KL than an MLP (0.0827 vs.\
0.0882), yet higher complete-candidate KL on the same teacher-proposed
support (0.1020 vs.\ 0.0768). This counterexample to a monotonic
relationship motivates directly supervising complete candidates;
it does not by itself establish a recommendation-quality reversal.

Two structures make those candidates useful training targets.
First, items with shared SID prefixes form nested groups.
HServe sums complete-candidate probabilities within each prefix
group and matches the resulting marginals across SID depths.
Their role is to reweight optimization over granularities, not
to create independent teacher targets. Second, a complete-pair
score difference is the sum of depth-wise differences; signed
teacher--student residuals can offset each other. For each pair,
DA-CDRS measures how substituting one teacher increment changes a
pairwise ranking surrogate and uses positive improvements as
detached weights for increment matching.

Both losses operate offline on teacher-proposed candidates and
update the same student. The deployed model continues to encode
history once and generate valid SID extensions with its own heads.
We evaluate held-out Recall and NDCG rather than teacher agreement
alone. Our contributions are (1) diagnostics of the
gold-prefix/complete-candidate gap, (2) hierarchical preference
alignment on a shared candidate support, and (3) depth-attributed
relative-score supervision with controlled ablations.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Generative Recommendation with Semantic IDs}
TIGER \citep{tiger} popularized quantized SIDs and constrained
autoregressive item retrieval, building on discrete representation
learning and residual quantization \citep{vqvae,rqvae}.
Subsequent work explores learned tokenization and representations
\citep{actionpiece,letter,etegrec,idgenrec}, efficient identifier
design \citep{effitemid,practsid}, and the integration of
collaborative signals into language models \citep{lcrec,llmrecsurvey}.
Prefix-aware approaches
include RIPOR \citep{ripor} for retrieval ranking and APAO
\citep{apao} for mitigating premature beam pruning. LOHRec
\citep{lohrec} exploits order and hierarchy in quantized identifiers.
Complementary directions include long identifier generation and
unified generative retrieval and ranking \citep{rpg,liger,onerec}.
Unlike direct prefix-survival objectives, HServe distills probability
marginals induced by a shared distribution over complete SID items.

\subsection{Efficient Generative Recommendation}
Speculative decoding \citep{specdec,specsample} and blockwise
prediction \citep{blockwise} reduce autoregressive decoding cost.
Recommendation-specific approaches further study accelerated
retrieval \citep{specgr,atspeed}. Another direction uses compact
students to reduce per-step computation without verification.
SID-MLP \citep{sidmlp} predicts SIDs using lightweight heads;
\method\ preserves its serving architecture, applying structural
supervision during training only.

\subsection{Knowledge Distillation for Recommendation}
Knowledge distillation transfers predictions or sequences from
stronger models to smaller students \citep{hintonkd,seqkd,distilbert}.
Earlier implicit-feedback recommenders employ personalized ranking
and learned interaction functions \citep{bpr,ncf}.
Recommendation distillation transfers ranked items or relative
orderings: RD \citep{rankdistill}, RRD \citep{rrd}, and
RCE-KD \citep{rcekd} exemplify these alternatives. Generative
distillation includes on-policy GKD \citep{gkd} and contrastive
DistiLLM-2 \citep{distillm}. More directly, SID-MLP \citep{sidmlp}
distills SID teachers into compact architectures, while SmartGR
\citep{smartgr} distills SID hierarchy and beam-level ranking.
\method\ instead derives prefix marginals from a shared distribution
over complete candidates and weights increment matching by detached
single-depth changes to a complete-pair evaluation.

\section{Preliminaries}
\label{sec:prelim}

\subsection{SID Recommendation and Verify-Free Students}
\label{sec:prelim-models}

Let $u$ denote a recommendation request with history $x_u$,
and let item $i$ have a four-position SID
$z^i=(z_1^i,\ldots,z_4^i)$ in a 256-way codebook at each position.
The fixed TIGER teacher consists of a T5 history encoder $E_T$
and an autoregressive decoder that provides offline supervision.
All students reuse $E_T$ and frozen SID embeddings. Their trainable
reader $R_\phi$ (SID-MLP cross-attention, GRU, or temporal CNN)
operates on the encoder states, followed by the \emph{same kind}
of four prefix-conditioned MLP prediction heads $g_{\psi_d}$:
\begin{equation}
H_T(u)=E_T(x_u),\quad h_S(u)=R_\phi(H_T(u)),
\label{eq:student-flow}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\ell_{S,d}(u,p)=g_{\psi_d}(h_S(u),\operatorname{Emb}(p)),
\label{eq:student-head}
\end{equation}
where $p=z_{<d}$ is the already generated SID prefix. The
reader and heads are trainable; the encoder and SID embeddings
are fixed.

Digit cross-entropy uses the raw 256-way conditional
$q^{\mathrm{raw}}_{S,d}=\operatorname{softmax}(\ell_{S,d})$.
For candidate scoring and serving, let $\mathcal V_d(p)$ be
the legal extensions of prefix $p$; the student uses
\begin{equation}
q^{\mathrm{valid}}_{S,d}(c\mid u,p)=
\frac{q^{\mathrm{raw}}_{S,d}(c\mid u,p)
\mathbf1[c\in\mathcal V_d(p)]}
{\sum_{a\in\mathcal V_d(p)}
q^{\mathrm{raw}}_{S,d}(a\mid u,p)}.
\label{eq:valid-conditional}
\end{equation}
The implemented student leaves the first position at its raw
256-way softmax and applies legal-child masking at positions 2--4.
Consequently the same logits underlie two different probability
normalizations; CE and valid-candidate scoring are not interchangeable.
The student score and its depth increments are
\begin{equation}
s_S(i\mid u)=\sum_{d=1}^{4}
\log q^{\mathrm{valid}}_{S,d}(z_d^i\mid u,z_{<d}^i).
\label{eq:serving-scores}
\end{equation}
For the teacher, $t_d(i\mid u)$ denotes its cached SID
log-probability increment and $s_T^{\mathrm{SID}}=\sum_dt_d$.
The beam cache also provides its completed-candidate score
$s_T^{\mathrm{cache}}$, used to rank and normalize the teacher
candidate list; a cached termination contribution, if any,
is not silently equated with the four SID increments.
HServe uses these cached complete scores, whereas DA matches
the recorded depth increments. These definitions make their
shared candidate support explicit without assuming the
two teacher score conventions are identical.

The student decodes legal SIDs with constrained beam search
and no online teacher-decoder call. The teacher candidate list
is filtered to the same SID inventory; the student and teacher
need not have used identical beam-search procedures.

\subsection{Gold-Prefix Token Distillation Baseline}
\label{sec:prelim-base}

We consider a standard student trained with ground-truth SID
cross-entropy and token-level knowledge distillation:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{base}}
&=\mathcal L_{\mathrm{digit}}
+\lambda_{\mathrm{tok}}\mathcal L_{\mathrm{tok}},\\
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{digit}}
&=-\sum_{d=1}^{L}\log q_{S,d}^{\mathrm{raw}}(z_d^{\mathrm{gt}}\mid u,z_{<d}^{\mathrm{gt}}),\\
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{tok}}
&=\sum_{d=1}^{L}\KL\!\left(
p_T^{\mathrm{raw}}(\cdot\mid u,z_{<d}^{\mathrm{gt}})
\,\|\,
q_{S,d}^{\mathrm{raw}}(\cdot\mid u,z_{<d}^{\mathrm{gt}})
\right).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:base-obj}
\end{equation}

Here $z^{\mathrm{gt}}$ is the observed next-item SID and
$\lambda_{\mathrm{tok}}$ controls the distillation strength.
Importantly, token KD matches the entire next-code distribution at
each gold-path prefix, not merely the probability of the correct code.
Its direct targets nevertheless remain conditional predictions at
visited prefixes. Because alternative complete candidates may follow
prefixes absent from the observed path, matching these local
distributions does not directly constrain their joint ranking. We use
this objective to define the token-distilled baseline; ServeSID
retains the ground-truth term but replaces the token-KL term with its
structure-aware objectives.

\subsection{Two Structural Views of SID Scoring}
\label{sec:prelim-structure}

\textbf{Hierarchical candidate organization.} A prefix $p$ of length
$d$ identifies the group
\begin{equation}
\mathcal C^{(d)}(p)=\{i\in\mathcal I:z_{1:d}^{i}=p\}.
\label{eq:prefix-group}
\end{equation}
If $p'$ extends $p$, then
$\mathcal C^{(d')}(p')\subseteq\mathcal C^{(d)}(p)$. Thus, complete
candidates form nested groups across SID depths. On a fixed candidate
support, the sum of complete-item preference probabilities within a
group defines its prefix marginal. These marginals are mutually
consistent because each complete SID belongs to exactly one prefix
group at any depth. These groups are induced by SID codes and need not
coincide with externally defined semantic product categories.

\textbf{Additive relative-score composition.} For two complete
candidates $i^+$ and $i^-$, define the score gap for
$m\in\{T,S\}$ as
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
G_m &= s_m(i^+\mid u)-s_m(i^-\mid u)\\
&=\sum_{d=1}^{L}\Delta G_m^{(d)},
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:gap-decomp}
\end{equation}
where $\Delta G_m^{(d)}$ is the conditional log-probability
difference at SID position $d$, not a network layer.
Scores for a shared prefix cancel in deterministic evaluation,
while later increments can have opposite signs.
Their sum determines the final ordering. These two structures
motivate HServe and DA-CDRS.

\section{Diagnosis: Structural Limitations of Token-Level Distillation}
\label{sec:diagnosis}

We analyze token-distilled MLP, GRU, and CNN students on Amazon
Beauty, Toys, and Video Games using 10{,}000 held-out requests per
dataset. The teacher's top-40 valid complete SIDs define a fixed
support for comparing teacher and student scores. No held-out teacher
outputs enter training.

\subsection{Hierarchical Candidate Preference Mismatch}
\label{sec:diag-h1}

Gold-prefix token KL evaluates next-token agreement on the observed
SID path, while complete-candidate KL compares normalized preferences
over the shared support. At prefix depth $d$, we measure hierarchical
drift as
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{HDrift}_d(u)=
\KL\!\left(
\pi_{T,40}^{(d)}(\cdot\mid u)
\;\|\;
\pi_{S,40}^{(d)}(\cdot\mid u)
\right),
\label{eq:hdrift}
\end{equation}
where each marginal sums the probabilities of supported candidates
sharing a prefix. Preferred-prefix disagreement at depth $d$ is the
fraction of held-out requests for which teacher and student select
different highest-mass depth-$d$ prefix groups on this support.

\textbf{Local-to-candidate preference mismatch.} Gold-prefix token KD aligns conditional distributions along observed SID paths but does not directly match preferences over complete candidates. Figure~\ref{fig:stat-h1}(a) shows that lower token KL need not imply lower candidate-level KL: on Video Games, GRU has lower token KL than MLP ($0.0827$ vs.\ $0.0882$) but higher candidate KL ($0.1020$ vs.\ $0.0768$). This motivates explicit complete-candidate distribution alignment.

\textbf{Hierarchical prefix-preference mismatch.} Shared SID prefixes define nested candidate groups whose probability masses are aggregated from complete candidates (Figure~\ref{fig:hierarchical}). Figure~\ref{fig:stat-h1}(b) shows preferred-prefix disagreement rates of $13.4\%$--$42.8\%$ at depth one and $31.9\%$--$67.7\%$ at depth two. Since exact candidate-distribution matching also determines prefix marginals, hierarchical alignment adds direct multi-granularity optimization constraints rather than independent teacher information.

\subsection{Directionally Mixed Cross-Depth Residuals}
\label{sec:diag-d2}

We examine teacher--student relative-score residuals
$r_d=\Delta G_T^{(d)}-\Delta G_S^{(d)}$ on held-out teacher-ordered
candidate pairs. A mixed-sign pair has both a positive and a negative
residual across eligible non-final SID depths; the cancellation ratio
summarizes how signed depth residuals offset one another. Figure~\ref{fig:stat-d12} shows that $75.4\%$--$86.3\%$ of pairs contain both positive and negative depth-wise residuals, while the largest absolute residual is negative in $47.6\%$--$51.3\%$ of cases. Thus, discrepancy magnitude alone does not identify corrections that favor the teacher-preferred ordering. This motivates direction-aware allocation of depth-wise distillation supervision.

Together, hierarchical candidate-preference mismatches and directionally mixed depth residuals motivate the two complementary structural objectives introduced next.

\section{\method: Hierarchy-Aligned and Decision-Attributed Distillation}
\label{sec:method}

The frozen teacher proposes complete SID candidates on a common
support. HServe aligns their prefix marginals; DA-CDRS scores
single-depth teacher replacements to allocate relative-score
distillation. Both losses update the same student.

\input{Figures/fig_method_overview.tex}

\subsection{Overall Training Objective}
\label{sec:method-total}

For a training request $u$ with observed next-item SID
$z^{\mathrm{gt}}$, our objective is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{\method}}
=\mathcal L_{\mathrm{digit}}
+\lambda_H\mathcal L_{\mathrm{HServe}}
+\lambda_D\mathcal L_{\mathrm{DA\text{-}CDRS}},
\label{eq:total}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal L_{\mathrm{digit}}$ is the ground-truth SID
cross-entropy defined in Section~\ref{sec:prelim-base}, and
$\lambda_H,\lambda_D\ge0$ control the structural objectives. Unlike
the baseline, \method\ does not retain a separate gold-prefix
token-KL term. Ground-truth supervision remains necessary because
matching a teacher's preferences does not guarantee relevance to the
observed next item. The following losses operate on teacher-proposed
complete candidates, with gradients applied only to the student.
Digit CE and HServe average over training requests; DA instead averages over all valid candidate pairs in the minibatch. The teacher remains frozen.

\subsection{HServe: Hierarchical Serving-Distribution Alignment}
\label{sec:method-hserve}

\textbf{Shared complete-candidate support.} For each training
request, we obtain the teacher's top-$M$ beam-reachable, valid
complete SIDs and cache the resulting support $\C_M(u)$. The teacher supplies cached complete-candidate scores
$s_T^{\mathrm{cache}}$; the student recomputes valid-prefix scores
$s_S$ from Eq.~\ref{eq:serving-scores}. Both are normalized on the
same finite candidate support. Teacher cached scores can include
terms not present in the four recorded SID increments (e.g., EOS),
so HServe's distributional comparison uses the cached totals,
while DA uses the four recorded increments. We define a distribution normalized within
this support:
\begin{equation}
\pi_{m,M}(i\mid u)=
\frac{\exp(s_m(i\mid u))}
{\sum_{j\in\C_M(u)}\exp(s_m(j\mid u))},
\qquad i\in\C_M(u).
\label{eq:item-dist}
\end{equation}
This normalization makes teacher and student probabilities comparable
over identical candidates. It represents preference
conditional on the selected support rather than over the entire item
catalog, so candidates outside $\C_M(u)$ are not part of this KL
objective; using a common support avoids interpreting differences
caused merely by two models proposing different candidate lists as
distributional mismatch.

\textbf{Hierarchically consistent prefix marginals.} As illustrated
in Figure~\ref{fig:hierarchical}, shared SID prefixes form nested
candidate groups whose preference mass may differ between teacher and
student. A length-$d$ prefix $p$ defines the subset of supported
candidates whose first $d$ codes equal $p$. We aggregate their complete-candidate
probabilities:
\begin{equation}
\pi_{m,M}^{(d)}(p\mid u)=
\sum_{\substack{i\in\C_M(u)\\z_{1:d}^i=p}}
\pi_{m,M}(i\mid u).
\label{eq:prefix-marginal}
\end{equation}
Every candidate contributes to exactly one prefix group at each
depth, so the marginals are normalized and mutually consistent across
depths. At $d=L$, each distinct complete SID corresponds to a leaf
and the marginal reduces to the complete-candidate distribution. At
shallower depths, it captures the total preference mass assigned to
groups of candidates with common initial codes. No auxiliary prefix
predictor is introduced: all marginals are deterministic aggregations
of the same complete-candidate scores. These groupings are defined by
code identity rather than manually assigned product categories.

\textbf{Multi-level KL.} HServe aligns teacher and student
prefix distributions at each SID depth:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{HServe}}(u)=
\sum_{d=1}^{L}\alpha_d\,
\KL\!\left(\pi_{T,M}^{(d)}\,\|\,\pi_{S,M}^{(d)}\right),
\quad \alpha_d\ge0.
\label{eq:hserve}
\end{equation}
The leaf term ($d=L$) directly aligns complete-item preferences,
addressing the local-to-candidate gap. Shallower terms align probability
mass over shared-prefix groups, addressing hierarchical drift.
Exact leaf-level matching implies agreement at all shallower depths;
these terms provide explicit multi-granularity optimization constraints
rather than independent teacher information. For equal depth weights, the KL chain rule assigns conditional
prefix divergences at depths 1--4 effective weights $(4,3,2,1)$,
rather than the leaf-only $(1,1,1,1)$; these are KL terms induced
by the \emph{supported candidate distribution}, not the decoder's
untruncated token KL.

\subsection{DA-CDRS: Decision-Attributed Cross-Depth
Relative-Score Distillation}
\label{sec:method-dacdrs}

\textbf{Cross-cutoff candidate pairs.} We form teacher-ordered pairs
from the fixed candidate list:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal B(u)=\{(i^+,i^-):
\operatorname{rank}_T(i^+\mid u)\le K_{\mathrm{tr}},\;
K_{\mathrm{tr}}<\operatorname{rank}_T(i^-\mid u)\le B\},
\label{eq:pairs}
\end{equation}
where $B\le M$. Pair labels describe teacher preferences, not
ground-truth relevance. Define the per-position increment as
$a_{T,d}(i)=t_d(i\mid u)$ for the teacher and
$a_{S,d}(i)=\log q^{\mathrm{valid}}_{S,d}
(z_d^i\mid u,z_{<d}^i)$ for the student.
For the candidate pair $b=(i^+,i^-)$,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\Delta G_m^{(d)}(b)&=a_{m,d}(i^+)-a_{m,d}(i^-),\\
G_m(b)&=\sum_{d=1}^{L}\Delta G_m^{(d)}(b).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:pairs-gap}
\end{equation}
Thus $G_T$ is the \emph{four-SID-increment} gap, not
necessarily the gap between cached teacher totals; pair identities
follow the teacher's cached ranking. The two coincide if the
cached score remainder is constant across a request's candidates.

\textbf{Decision-attributed depth weighting.} The signed residual is
\begin{equation}
r_d(b)=\Delta G_T^{(d)}(b)-\Delta G_S^{(d)}(b).
\label{eq:residual}
\end{equation}
Shared-prefix increments cancel before the first divergent position
$d_{\mathrm{div}}(b)$. We restrict DA corrections to non-final depths:
\begin{equation}
v_d(b)=\mathbf1[d\ge d_{\mathrm{div}}(b)]
       \mathbf1[d<L].
\label{eq:valid-depth}
\end{equation}
The final SID depth still contributes to the complete score gap but
is not directly corrected by DA-CDRS. As illustrated in
Figure~\ref{fig:ranking_depth}, each hypothetical replacement keeps all
other student increments fixed:
\begin{equation}
\widetilde G_{S,d}(b)=G_S(b)-\Delta G_S^{(d)}(b)
+\Delta G_T^{(d)}(b).
\label{eq:cf-gap}
\end{equation}
We evaluate the complete-pair ordering with
$\ell_{\mathrm{pair}}(G)=\operatorname{softplus}(\mu-G)$,
where $\mu$ is a fixed margin. The positive reduction in this
\emph{evaluation function} defines attribution:
\begin{equation}
A_d(b)=v_d(b)\left[
\ell_{\mathrm{pair}}(G_S(b))-
\ell_{\mathrm{pair}}(\widetilde G_{S,d}(b))
\right]_+.
\label{eq:attribution}
\end{equation}
Detached weights normalize these improvements:
\begin{equation}
w_d(b)=\operatorname{stopgrad}\left(
\frac{A_d(b)}{\sum_{t=1}^{L-1}A_t(b)+\epsilon}
\right).
\label{eq:weights}
\end{equation}
The evaluation is nonlinear in the complete student gap.
For a small positive $r_d$,
$A_d=\sigma(\mu-G_S)r_d+O(r_d^2)$; its common first-order
factor cancels in normalized $w_d$, which then approaches
normalized positive-residual weighting. Larger residuals can
yield different weights. These score replacements attribute
changes in a ranking surrogate, not causal effects of
parameter interventions; the surrogate is not itself optimized.

\textbf{Scale-normalized correction.} With
depth-specific standard deviations computed from valid \emph{training}
pairs, we set
\begin{equation}
\sigma_d=\max\left(
\operatorname{Std}_{\mathrm{train,valid}}[
\Delta G_T^{(d)}],10^{-4}\right).
\label{eq:scale}
\end{equation}
DA-CDRS optimizes only weighted Huber increment matching:
\begin{equation}
\ell_{\mathrm{DA}}(b)=
\lambda_{\mathrm{corr}}\sum_{d=1}^{L-1}
w_d(b)\,\rho_H\!\left(
\frac{\Delta G_S^{(d)}(b)-\Delta G_T^{(d)}(b)}{\sigma_d}
\right).
\label{eq:correction}
\end{equation}
The minibatch DA loss averages over all valid candidate pairs
across its training requests; requests without eligible pairs
contribute no term. If all eligible attributions vanish, the pair
adds no DA correction. The complete-pair Softplus is used \emph{only} to
compute detached weights, never as an independent training loss.
The Plain Pair ablation instead directly optimizes the same
Softplus evaluation on teacher-ordered candidates.

\subsection{Offline Training and Verify-Free Inference}
\label{sec:method-train-inf}

We construct teacher proposal lists, candidate scores, pair relations,
and teacher increment statistics from training requests only. The frozen teacher's data are cached; student scores, marginals, replacements, and attribution weights are computed during optimization. No validation or test request contributes teacher targets to the training loss.

At deployment, all teacher-dependent caches and distillation
computations are discarded. The student encodes history once and retains
depth-wise SID prediction, validity masking, and constrained
beam expansion. Consequently, the deployed student retains the original verify-free inference graph without introducing teacher-dependent scoring, auxiliary prediction heads, or reranking stages. The proposed objectives modify only the offline training procedure and do not constitute a new decoding algorithm. Cache construction, additional student scoring, and their storage
costs are reported separately from online serving in
Section~\ref{sec:exp-cost}. The method targets recommendation quality under an
unchanged verify-free inference graph, not a guaranteed preservation
of every teacher Top-$K$ item.

\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:exp}

We investigate three questions: (RQ1) Does structure-aware
distillation improve recommendation across datasets and students?
(RQ2) Does HServe improve on leaf-only complete-candidate matching?
(RQ3) Does depth attribution outperform uniform weighting and
direct pair-ranking supervision when combined with HServe?

\subsection{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:exp-setup}

\textbf{Datasets and evaluation.} We use Amazon Beauty and Toys from
Amazon Reviews 2014 and Video Games from Amazon Reviews 2023. User
interactions are ordered chronologically, with leave-one-out next-item
evaluation and at most 20 recent interactions retained as request
history. All methods within a dataset share identical preprocessing, splits, SID assignments, and validity constraints. We report Recall@5/10 and NDCG@5/10 against the held-out next item. Recall@$K$ measures whether the held-out next item appears among the top-$K$ recommendations, while NDCG@$K$ additionally accounts for its ranking position. These relevance-based metrics assess recommendation utility independently of teacher--student preference agreement.

\textbf{Teacher and students.} A frozen TIGER T5
encoder--autoregressive-decoder model provides supervision in
an RQ-VAE SID space (four 256-way codes per item).
Students retain its frozen history encoder and SID embeddings,
but replace the teacher decoder with an attention-based SID-MLP,
GRU (hidden 384), or CNN (256-channel) context reader and
four prefix-conditioned MLP heads. The \emph{total trainable}
reader-plus-head budgets are 1.664M, 2.304M, and 1.745M,
respectively; the frozen modules are excluded. Students
share the teacher's SID inventory and employ constrained beam search
with width 50; the teacher's beam-20 results are reference measurements rather than matched-cost comparisons. Fixing the SID inventory and validity constraints ensures that differences among training objectives are not attributable to changes in item tokenization or admissible candidate sequences. Student beam width is held constant within each architecture.  

\textbf{Training configuration.} For every training request, the
offline teacher cache contains up to $M=64$ valid complete SIDs and
their accumulated scores. HServe aligns distributions on this shared
support with depth weights $\alpha_d=1$ and $\lambda_H=4$. DA-CDRS samples teacher-ordered pairs between ranks 1--10 and
11--40. The complete-pair Softplus evaluation uses $\mu=0.05$ only
for detached attribution weights, with $\lambda_D=1$ and
$\lambda_{\mathrm{corr}}=31.42$. The uniform variant replaces these
weights with equal weights over eligible non-final SID depths;
neither variant directly optimizes an additional pairwise loss.
For the ablation-only \emph{HServe + Plain Pair} control, we remove DA-CDRS
and directly minimize the same complete-pair Softplus score on the
same teacher-ordered pairs alongside digit CE and HServe. Its pair
coefficient is $\lambda_P=1$.
We use learning rate $5\times10^{-5}$ and random seeds 42, 43, 44. All teacher scores, pairs, and
increment-scale statistics originate from training requests only;
validation and test teacher outputs are used exclusively for
independent diagnostics. The base KD objective combines digit CE with
gold-prefix token KL, whereas the \method\ objective combines digit CE
with HServe and DA-CDRS without an additional token-KL term. Checkpoint selection uses validation NDCG@10, without selecting on test metrics.

\textbf{Baselines.} Table~\ref{tab:main-all} evaluates adapted
RD, RRD, RCE-KD, GKD, DistiLLM-2, and SmartGR objectives on
the same frozen teacher, SID inventory, and student backbones.
The integrated appendix gives the common training objective,
candidate protocol and adaptation choices for these baselines.

\subsection{Overall Recommendation Performance}
\label{sec:exp-results}

Table~\ref{tab:main-all} reports held-out recommendation metrics; full
mean/SD summaries appear in the Supplement. Compared with base KD,
\method\ improves NDCG@10 for all nine dataset--student settings:
3.7\%, 2.8\%, and 15.4\% for Beauty MLP/GRU/CNN; 4.8\%, 12.8\%,
and 18.8\% for Toys; and 3.5\%, 2.6\%, and 10.4\% for Video Games.
Recall@10 also increases from 0.0580 to 0.0686 on Beauty--CNN and
0.0870 to 0.0944 on Video Games--CNN. Against the strongest reported non-\method\ NDCG@10 baseline
in each setting, the relative gain is 1.61\%--5.13\%.
The largest base-KD gains occur with CNN students.
Aggregate SDs alone do not establish paired significance.

\subsection{Ablation Study}
\label{sec:exp-ablation}

Table~\ref{tab:ablation} isolates the two structural objectives.
HServe-only outperforms leaf-only KL in all 36 reported metric
comparisons (e.g., Toys--CNN NDCG@10: 0.0320 vs.\ 0.0297).
Direct Softplus optimization (\emph{HServe + Plain Pair}) does not
consistently improve HServe-only: 10 metrics improve, 4 tie, and 22
decline. Full \method\ exceeds both Plain Pair and uniform depth
weighting in all 36 comparisons; Video Games--GRU NDCG@10 is
0.0506, 0.0510, and 0.0512, respectively. Unlike Plain Pair,
DA-CDRS uses Softplus only for detached attribution weights and
optimizes depth-wise Huber correction, not a direct pairwise loss.
Full \method\ also exceeds both single-objective variants.
Together, these comparisons demonstrate that the
decision-attributed depth weighting improves upon uniform
weighting and direct pairwise supervision in the tested settings.

\subsection{Measured Cost and Serving Scope}
\label{sec:exp-cost}

On Beauty, Base/Full serving throughput is 2769/3036,
3445/3423, and 3028/3024 requests/s for MLP, GRU, and CNN
(batch 32, beam 50). Despite the shared inference graph,
MLP differs by 9.6\% across the independent runs; measured
throughput is not an architectural invariant. Timings include
the frozen T5 history encoder, not just SID heads.
The MLP training-cost measurement at $M=64$ gives
4.6/11.5\,s per training epoch and 27/49\,min for complete
200-epoch Base/Full runs; peak allocated training memory rises
from 7.4 to 9.2--9.5\,GB. These offline timings are
measured at the stated $M=64$ support width; $B=40$
is the separate DA-CDRS outsider cutoff. Cache-export
beam width 40 is a distinct generation setting. The Supplement
documents the cache, timing phases, and distinct measurement
boundaries. No new online decoder, teacher-scoring stage, or
reranker is introduced by \method.

\section{Conclusion and Limitations}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We study structured preference distillation for lightweight SID
recommendation. HServe directly aligns complete-candidate and
consistent shared-prefix distributions; DA-CDRS allocates depth-wise
Huber matching through detached complete-pair evaluation
improvements. The jointly trained student retains frozen-encoder,
verify-free-decoder inference. Current evaluation is restricted to one teacher family,
one SID tokenizer, and three product domains. Teacher-proposed
supports may omit relevant items, and the attribution is not causal;
comparison with positive-residual weighting and broader deployment
tests remain future work.
